Step 0: Problem Framing and Scope Definition

⸻

1. Task Definition

The objective of this work is to identify and classify the dominant affective–ethical emotion expressed in classical Indian poetic verses written in Hindi, Tamil, and Bengali. Each poem is assumed to convey a single primary emotional or moral dominance, which is annotated in the dataset. The task is formulated as a single-label, multi-class classification problem, where the model predicts the dominant emotion by incorporating contextual cues such as devotion, morality, nature, and symbolism, in alignment with Indian aesthetic semantics.

⸻

2. Label Policy
	•	The dataset contains 46 unique primary labels, representing fine-grained emotional, moral, and philosophical states.
	•	All labels are retained without merging or relabeling.
	•	Each poem is associated with exactly one dominant label.
	•	The classification task is treated as single-label, multi-class prediction.
	•	Class imbalance is handled during training and evaluation using appropriate metrics and weighting strategies.
	•	The ground-truth labels provided in the dataset are considered authoritative.

⸻

3. Alignment with Indian Aesthetic (Rasa) Theory

Although the dataset labels are not explicitly defined using classical Indian aesthetic frameworks such as Navarasa or Bhakti Rasa, they can be interpreted as manifestations of broader Indian aesthetic emotions. The model predicts fine-grained dominant affective–ethical states, which are mapped post-hoc to corresponding Rasa categories only for interpretability and analysis. This mapping is not used during training or prediction, ensuring that the learning process remains faithful to the dataset while still aligning conceptually with Indian aesthetic theory.

⸻

4. Contextual Dimensions

To incorporate culturally grounded context, four contextual dimensions are defined and used for interpretation and modeling:
	1.	Devotion Context
Represents spiritual surrender, reverence, worship, and expressions of faith or bhakti.
	2.	Morality Context
Captures ethical duty, righteousness (dharma), responsibility, integrity, and moral conduct.
	3.	Nature Context
Reflects the use of natural imagery such as landscapes, seasons, celestial elements, and nature-based metaphors to convey emotional states.
	4.	Symbolism Context
Accounts for abstract, metaphorical, and philosophical expressions where emotions are conveyed indirectly through symbolic or conceptual language.

These dimensions are used to support context-aware emotion interpretation rather than replacing the primary classification objective.

⸻

5. Scope Clarification and Non-Claims

The proposed system does not:
	•	Directly predict Navarasa or Bhakti Rasa labels.
	•	Redefine, merge, or modify the provided dataset labels.
	•	Translate poems into another language for modeling.
	•	Claim full symbolic or theological interpretation of poetic content.
	•	Replace human literary or cultural analysis.

The system is designed as a computational approximation that supports dominant emotion classification through contextual modeling, not as a complete literary or philosophical interpreter.
